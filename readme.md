# Breakthrough Scheme for 6G ISAC: PRS and PDSCH Overlay, Solving the Double Bottlenecks of Spectral Efficiency and Perception Ambiguity

Personal Blog: https://www.cnblogs.com/EWFrontier  
✿6G ISAC Breakthrough Solution: PRS and PDSCH Overlay, Solving the Double Bottleneck of Spectral Efficiency and Perception Ambiguity ✿In the wave of 6G communication technology evolution, Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) is recognized as a core empowerment technology—it breaks the resource barrier between communication and perception, allowing wireless networks to not only carry high-speed data transmission but also achieve target detection, positioning, and other perceptive functions, providing key support for emerging scenarios such as autonomous driving, intelligent security, and the Internet of Things. Telecom operators generally expect that 6G ISAC can be based on the existing 5G NR infrastructure for upgrades, achieving dual improvements in resource utilization efficiency and service quality while minimizing network transformation costs. Recently, a study published at the 2025 IEEE CCNC conference proposed an innovative solution for the Positioning Reference Signal (PRS) and Physical Downlink Shared Channel (PDSCH) overlay transmission, successfully solving three core issues of spectrum resource waste, perception distance ambiguity, and signal interference. This provides an important technical path for the engineering implementation of 6G ISAC.  
Research Background: Core Challenges and Industry Demands of 6G ISAC With the explosive growth of applications such as autonomous driving, health monitoring, and intelligent inspection, wireless communication systems face dual pressures: they need to meet the high-speed data transmission needs of massive devices while also possessing precise target perception capabilities. In traditional solutions, communication and perception usually occupy independent spectrum resources, leading to low spectral utilization; if reusing existing signal resources, two major problems arise:
1. The issue of signal interference. When the sensed signals (such as PRS) share frequency and time resources with communication signals (such as PDSCH), they can interfere with each other, leading to an increase in communication error rates and a decrease in perception accuracy, severely affecting service quality (QoS).
2. The problem of distance ambiguity. The periodic structure of PRS can cause "ghost targets" during the perception process, where the system mistakenly classifies false signals as real targets, which is a fatal flaw for safety-sensitive scenarios such as autonomous driving obstacle avoidance and security intrusion detection.
Previous research has attempted various solutions, but all have significant shortcomings: some alleviate ambiguity by cooperating multiple reference signals, without considering the interference between communication and perception; others reduce the resource occupation of sensed signals while sacrificing the maximum detectable distance; still others have extremely high computational complexity, making it difficult to meet the engineering requirements of 3GPP standards. Therefore, developing a technology solution that is compatible with 3GPP standards and balances spectrum efficiency and perception accuracy has become a core need in the industry.
Core solution: Integrated design of signal superposition and intelligent algorithms. To address these challenges, a research team from the University of Paris-Saclay and Nokia's standard department proposed an integrated solution called "PRS and PDSCH superposition transmission + interference elimination + ambiguity suppression." The core idea is to fully reuse the existing signal resources of 5G NR, achieving coordinated optimization of communication and perception through meticulous design.
1. Signal Overlap: The core innovation of the efficient reuse scheme for time-frequency resources lies in the overlap transmission of the PDSCH for communication and the PRS for perception within the OFDM resource grid. The choice of these two signals is due to their inherent synergistic advantages: the PRS, serving as the positioning reference signal for 5G NR, possesses abundant time-frequency resources (supporting 24-272 physical resource block (PRB) allocations) and flexible configuration modes (four comb structures and five symbol configurations), exhibiting strong noise resistance and good autocorrelation, making it an ideal carrier for perception; whereas the PDSCH, being the core data transmission channel of 5G NR, its accompanying demodulation reference signal (DMRS) can precisely complete channel estimation, providing crucial support for interference elimination.
To achieve optimal resource allocation, the scheme employs a dynamic power allocation strategy, adjusting the proportion of resources between the two through parameters γₛ (perception power weight) and γ_c (communication power weight), ensuring that γₛ + γ_c = 1. This design allows for "on-demand" allocation of time-frequency resources between communication and perception, fundamentally changing the state of resource fragmentation in traditional schemes and significantly enhancing spectral efficiency.
2. Interference Elimination: The precise channel compensation signal superimposed by DMRS empowered interference removal inevitably leads to mutual interference, with the interference from PRS to PDSCH directly affecting the quality of data decoding. To address this issue, the team designed an interference removal algorithm based on DMRS: first, utilize DMRS to perform precise channel estimation, obtaining a channel response model; then, based on this model, reconstruct the received signal of PRS without PDSCH; finally, subtract the reconstructed PRS signal from the actual received signal to obtain a pure PDSCH signal for data decoding. This process fully leverages the channel estimation capability of DMRS, significantly improving the accuracy of interference removal and safeguarding communication performance.
3. Shadowing suppression: The design of PRS comb structure (Comb 2/4/6/12) for different comb sizes is the key to distance shadowing - the periodic subcarrier distribution will cause false peaks in the system perception. Based on the characteristics of different comb sizes, the team proposes two targeted algorithms.
•  
Algorithm 1 (Applicable to Comb-2/4): Leveraging the complementary characteristics of false alarm target locations in PRS and DMRS, perform Hadamard product operation on their inverse fast Fourier transform (IFFT) results to mutually cancel false peaks, retaining true target signal.
•  
Algorithm 2 (Applicable to Comb-6/12): When the comb size is large, the false alarm targets of PRS and DMRS will partially overlap. At this time, by summing signals, normalizing processing, and then performing IFFT, the signal periodicity is disrupted, thereby suppressing the peak of false alarms.
Both algorithms do not sacrifice the maximum detection distance, perfectly balancing perception accuracy and coverage range.
Simulation Verification: Significant Performance Improvement, Compatible with 3GPP Standards. To validate the effectiveness of the solution, the research team developed a simulation platform that adheres to 3GPP standards using MATLAB's 5G toolbox. The platform was configured with scenario parameters closely aligned with real-world applications: both gNB and receiver equipped with an 8×8 antenna array. Two targets were positioned at 711m and 846m respectively, moving at speeds of 2m/s and 10m/s respectively. The center frequency was set at 25GHz, subcarrier spacing at 120kHz, and signal-to-noise ratio at 15dB.
Simulation results show that the performance of the proposed scheme has been significantly improved: in terms of communication, the interference cancellation algorithm reduces the bit error rate (BER) by up to 57% — when the PRS comb size is 2, the BER drops from 0.0121 without interference cancellation to 0.0052, while the throughput is greatly enhanced; in terms of perception, both algorithms can completely eliminate false alarms, and the estimation errors of the distance and velocity of real targets are kept within an extremely small range.
It is noteworthy that the proposal fully complies with core standards such as 3GPP TS 38.211 and TS 38.214, without requiring modifications to the existing 5G NR physical layer architecture, demonstrating strong potential for engineering implementation.
The core value of this research lies in its first implementation of standardized stacking of PRS and PDSCH within an OFDM resource grid, achieved through a combination of "resource reuse + intelligent algorithms." This approach simultaneously addresses three core issues: low spectral efficiency, distance ambiguity, and signal interference, providing a technical path for 6G ISAC that is both practical and economically viable.
In the future, the research team will further explore power allocation strategies under different signal-to-noise ratios and the trade-off between subcarrier spacing and perception accuracy and communication performance. Additionally, they plan to expand algorithm optimizations for multi-objective and non-line-of-sight (NLOS) scenarios, laying a more solid foundation for the large-scale application of 6G ISAC. This achievement not only provides new ideas for academic research but also demonstrates the feasibility of upgrading 5G infrastructure to support 6G ISAC based on existing 5G infrastructure, holding significant theoretical and engineering value.
